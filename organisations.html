For practices, PCNs, ICBs and partners

Patients who remember need to call back less.

Patients already record appointments, often covertly. In one UK survey, one in seven had secretly recorded an appointment. MyScribe makes it open, agreed and useful, with a faithful record of the conversation and a plain-words summary, both owned by the patient.

40-80%

of what is said in a medical appointment is forgotten almost straight away, and almost half of what is remembered is wrong. (Kessels, 2003)

1 in 5

GP appointments are for needs that aren't medical. The answer is often a service, a name or a number, and those are the easiest things to forget.

Less recontact

Fewer “what did the doctor say?” calls to reception, because the answer is on the patient's phone.

Better-informed patients

Plans, next steps, referrals and signposting in plain words, shared with the carers and family who help make them happen.

A transcript, not a tape

The audio is deleted once it's written down. Patients keep the full conversation, with anything unclear flagged, and MySummary links back to what was actually said.

Ask Your Appointment

“Was it one tablet or two?” Patients ask, and MyScribe takes them to the exact moment in the conversation, in the clinician's own words, to read or hear aloud.

Safe by design

MySummary sticks to what was said. It doesn't diagnose or advise, and it leaves out medicine names and doses, sending patients to the clinician's exact words instead. Every summary tells patients to check the full conversation or ask their clinician if anything is unclear.

Aligned with the 10 Year Health Plan

Patient power, neighbourhood health and care closer to home, in a tool people carry with them.
